## What the imagery shows and what remains uncertain

The current Park City mosaic is reconstructed from four NAIP items dated September 24 and November 21, 2021. In the imagery-only sheets, many windows contain large bright or blue-white regions consistent with snow or bright exposed surfaces. Their condition is not independently classified: do not label them snow from brightness alone. Keep snow, mixed cover, bare ground, cloud, deep shadow, canopy, bright bare rock/pavement, invalid and unknown as separate review tags. An image-only first pass highlights W001, W004, W006–W008, W014–W018 and W020/W022 as especially bright or snow-like in 2021; their September 2018 counterparts often show more exposed ground/vegetation. This supports checking snow/season as a visibility factor, but is not a measured snow rate or an independent tread score. Several 2018 chips also have dense canopy and deep shadows. Light lines may be trails, roads, or other tracks; the sheets alone cannot resolve that class. Two windows, W005 and W023, report only 23.0047% current-image validity in the paired screen, while the other 22 report full validity. Those two require source-footprint/mask investigation; do not describe the full current sample as fully covered.

A second, better-covered alternative is the September 2018 NAIP mosaic. The exact four STAC items are dated September 9 and 27, have 0.6 m GSD, use source EPSG:26912, and explicitly identify `eo:bands` as Red/Green/Blue/NIR. I fetched all 24 exact recovered-A windows and reprojected them to the same 0.6 m EPSG:32612 window bounds. All 24 returned nonzero RGB and masks reported full coverage. The rendered sheets show many 2018 scenes without the broad bright/blue-white appearance seen in several 2021 windows. They look like the stronger candidate for a blinded visibility review, but this does not establish that a light/dark line is a target trail, nor that the older trails are unchanged. The 2018 run logged a GDAL warning that an optional `WarpedVRT` resolution option was ignored; the per-window output transform is exactly the requested 0.6 m EPSG:32612 grid. Stable-landmark registration remains unmeasured, so pixel alignment is still open. The municipal PC2018 service (0.5 m, four-band U8, flown June 24 and July 1) remains secondary: 13/24 candidate RGB chips were entirely zero, with a 57.1854% mean zero-filled fraction despite masks reporting 100% validity. Do not use those areas in a comparison denominator.

The source-item query resolves band identity for Park City: all four reconstructed 2021 NAIP items and all four September 2018 items explicitly identify Red/Green/Blue/NIR under STAC `eo:bands` (the `raster:bands` extension is absent). The fetched 2021 COG has four bands and Rasterio tags band 4 `undefined`; the writer previously emitted `red,green,blue,alpha` by default. `fetch_naip` now explicitly writes RGB plus `undefined` for new outputs, and its regression test verifies unchanged channel values. This fixes the output color interpretation only. Existing rasters still use `nodata=0`; `tiles.py` reads four numeric bands directly and does not consult the color interpretation or source mask. The writer's zero-filled gaps, source masks and training tile validity therefore still need a source-to-tensor audit before changing data. Never use numeric zero alone as nodata or trust a full mask when returned RGB is black ([Planetary Computer NAIP example](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/docs/quickstarts/storage/)).

I retrieved the bounded 2018 chips from the completed Kaggle output and ran a local SIFT descriptor/RANSAC similarity screen against each current-image chip. None of 24 pairs met the screen criterion (at least 8 inliers within 2 pixels and at least 30% inlier fraction). This means the available image features did not verify alignment; it does not prove the rasters are misaligned because snow, canopy and repeated terrain can defeat automated matching. The paired images are not approved for training-input substitution or pixelwise model comparison until manually identified stable landmarks support the required one-pixel registration. Per-window counts and NPZ hashes are in `registration_screen.json`.

PC2018's public service metadata confirms the flight dates, 0.5 m pixels and four U8 bands, but does not name band roles or establish machine-learning reuse rights ([PC2018Imagery service](https://gis.parkcity.org/server/rest/services/PC2018Imagery/ImageServer)). All 24 sample centers falling inside its published extent did not ensure valid image pixels. The municipal source's use terms remain unknown.

## Labels and split limits

No independent, dated geometry has been adjudicated against imagery. UGRC is not independent for the existing Park City labels: the source inventory already includes 55 UGRC features. Park City points to Mountain Trails Foundation as its most accurate online local trail resource, but a dated downloadable GIS/as-built/maintenance layer, its overlap with existing vectors, positional uncertainty, trail construction/reroute dates and ML permission remain unverified. A public municipal WalkingRoutes layer's published extent is east of this sample AOI; its date and MTB-singletrack semantics are also unknown. Consequently there are zero verified target-trail or distractor samples and no trusted VAL reference set.

The current label code reveals why source-tag audit matters. Explicit OSM `footway=sidewalk` and `crossing` are excluded/ignored, but remaining footways and tracks can become positive path/track supervision; source tags and weights do not prove a line is recreational hiking or MTB tread. UGRC CartoCodes are mapped to path/track, and USFS motor-use tags distinguish some tracks while unknown motor-use gets lower path weight. Binary training merges trail types. A street or vehicle-only dirt road can therefore be a distractor if imported/tagged as a positive, while unrecorded background is not verified negative. We have not measured how often these cases occur in the actual A tensors; the audit must join source IDs/tags to the rasterized class, target and weight tensors before any edit.

Recovered A split membership is 1,745 train, 172 VAL and 358 held-out. The geometric pairwise cross-split footprint overlap sums to about 2.34 km², including 0.401 km² between Park City VAL and held-out. These are bounding-footprint areas; nodata-valid overlap was not established. Historical and replay F1 values measure agreement with the existing labels and do not prove independent spatial generalization.

Earlier audits remain hypotheses, not label corrections: the provisional imagery/relief check saw Park City relief-response offset median absolute 4.2 m over 500 samples (46.2% above 5 m; 24.2% above 10 m); the Corner Canyon inventory had 2,199 unnamed OSM footways totaling about 196.8 km of 2,229 features. Neither finding alone proves a trail label is wrong or right.

## Training decision and next work

All five candidate arms remain ineligible in `launch_decisions.json`. The A replay gate is met for published held-out pixel parity, but the following data gates remain open:

1. Trace the two partial-coverage 2021 windows, W005 and W023, through exact source footprints and masks. Audit `nodata=0` handling and record one source-to-training-tensor example; new NAIP downloads now have the corrected RGB+undefined color tag.
2. Conduct an image-only paired review of exact valid 2018/2021 windows, scoring snow, bare/mixed, shadow, cloud, canopy, saturation and tread visibility separately; measure stable-landmark registration. Keep all-zero/invalid areas out of comparison denominators.
3. Obtain dated independent Park City trail records and documented reuse permission; verify source lineage, tread type, positional accuracy and construction/reroute dates. Freeze trusted VAL geometry before exposing predictions.
4. Then run A VAL modality diagnostics and only reopen a training arm if the preregistered thresholds are met. Preserve snow/shadow/canopy as separate evaluation slices; do not drop snow-affected samples or let a date/brightness heuristic determine labels.
5. Keep the seven new sites at `review_later` until independent target/distractor sample counts, actual 3DEP raster coverage, seasonal image evidence and permissions are established. Do not add Bentonville or train on new areas under this plan.

**Recommended next action:** inspect why W005 and W023 have partial current imagery, then measure stable-landmark registration for the 24 paired NAIP windows and request dated independent Park City trail references. Do not launch training until label and weather gates are supported.
